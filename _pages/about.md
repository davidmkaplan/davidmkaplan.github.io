I am an Associate Professor in the School of Psychological Sciences at Macquarie University, where I serve as Head of Neuroscience Education.

My research sits at the intersection of **motor neuroscience**, **systems neuroscience**, and the **philosophy of neuroscience**. I study the computational processes underlying movement planning and sensorimotor learning, including how sensory uncertainty shapes learning and how expert motor skills develop in minimally invasive and robotic surgery.

Research themes
===============

### Sensorimotor learning

How do people plan, control, and adapt movement when sensory information is uncertain? My work examines the interacting contributions of feedforward planning, feedback control, and motor memory.

### Expertise and surgical performance

I investigate the sensorimotor challenges of minimally invasive and robotic surgery, with the aim of improving training and performance.

### Philosophy and methodology of neuroscience

My philosophical work addresses explanation, modelling, measurement, neural computation, and the use of model organisms in neuroscience.

Background
==========

I completed a PhD in Philosophy and Cognitive Neuroscience at Duke University in 2007. I then held postdoctoral fellowships in the Philosophy-Neuroscience-Psychology Program and the Department of Neuroscience at Washington University in St. Louis before joining Macquarie University.
